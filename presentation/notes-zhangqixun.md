## Slide 3 — What we rebuilt, and what we could not · 0:40

> We could not rebuild the paper's setup exactly, so we rebuilt the part that
> matters and wrote down everything else.
>
> Same idea, current tools: clang-14 on Ubuntu 22.04 under WSL2, unmodified —
> no patched runtime, because the paper's patch does not apply to compiler-rt
> from 2021. Five purpose-built C workloads instead of SPEC CPU2006, which is
> licensed and retired. Each workload is built three ways from one source:
> `base` at -O2, `asan` with reads and writes instrumented, and `asanw` with
> reads turned off, which is how we test the write-only claim.
>
> What we could not have: LLVM 3.1, the 2012 HP Z600, the 300 Chromium bugs,
> the Valgrind comparison. Twenty deviations are logged in
> `docs/deviations.md` — we list them before the numbers, not after.

Point at the three build boxes when you say "three ways from one source".
Leave the right-hand card alone unless asked; it is the honest half of the slide.

## Slide 4 — Making the measurement trustworthy · 0:35

> Two things had to be true before any number was worth reporting.
>
> First, the runs had to finish. Eleven of our first forty ASan runs died
> silently, no output at all, because ASLR collides with the shadow mapping.
> Every run now goes through `setarch -R`; zero of forty since.
>
> Second, the build had to still be a checker. `make doctor` plants one bug of
> each class and confirms the instrumented build still catches all four, and
> the checksums match the baseline — a fast sanitizer that misses the bug is
> worthless.
>
> That gave eighteen runs per point across four sessions, with the drift
> between sessions smaller than the noise inside one — which is why the error
> bars in the next two slides can be trusted.

## Prepared answers (Q&A, 2 minutes)

| Question | Answer, short | Where it is |
|---|---|---|
| Why not the real SPEC benchmarks? | Licensed and retired; five small workloads isolate the specific costs ASan adds, and we say so | D2, `bench/*.c` |
| Your mean is 3.32× but you quote 1.55× — which is it? | Both; `b1_allocs` is an allocation-only loop, so it is an upper bound on allocator cost and is excluded from the second mean | D15, `results/summary.md` |
| Why disable ASLR? Is that cheating? | Not a performance change; without it 11 of 40 runs crashed before printing a result | D14 |
| Why does VmPeak read 20 TB? | The 1/8 shadow reservation is `PROT_NONE` and counts as virtual memory; peak RSS is the comparable number | D13, backup B3 |
| Is 2.56× on `b4_float` real or just lost vectorisation? | Not vectorisation: already 2.54× at -O1 where clang vectorises nothing, and `-Rpass` shows the loop unvectorised at -O2 in both builds; it is the read-side shadow checks — write-only it is 1.26× | D20, `results/ext/opt-summary.md` |
| Why does the ratio change with -O level? | An unoptimised baseline spends its time on loads, stores and spills, so the same shadow checks are a smaller share: 1.39× at -O0, 1.78× at -O3 excluding b1 | D20, backup B4 |
| How noisy is this? | `b2_pointer` is the worst at ±15 % between sessions (1.31× ± 0.15); the rest are under 5 %; all timings are reported with sd | `results/variance.md` |
| n = 18 — pooled how? | Four sessions, 3 + 5 + 5 + 5 runs per point; between-session drift stayed below within-session noise | D19, `results/raw/` |
| Could the difference be clang-14, not the shadow memory? | Yes — that is our largest deviation (D3) and the honest limit of the study: separating them needs a build of LLVM 3.1 that no current distro ships | D3, `docs/report.md` §6 |
| Did you keep the "73 %" anywhere? | As an -O2 figure only; the report says it must not be quoted as a general ASan cost | `docs/report.md` §5 |
