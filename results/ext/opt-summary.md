## Checksum consistency

Every workload produced a single checksum across all four levels and both modes, so neither the optimisation level nor the instrumentation changed the computed result.

## Conclusion

**Hypothesis (a): supported.** Excluding `b1_allocs`, the mean slowdown is 1.39× at -O0 against 1.69× at -O2 (all five: 2.90× vs 3.53×; criterion: at least 10 % lower). An unoptimised baseline already spends its time on loads, stores and spills, so the added shadow checks are a smaller share of the total. The paper's 73 % is an -O2 figure and should not be read as independent of the optimisation level.

**Hypothesis (b): not supported.** `b4_float` goes from 2.77× at -O2 to 1.81× at -O0, while the mean of `b2`/`b3`/`b5` goes from 1.33× to 1.24×, so the ratio does shrink markedly at -O0. But at -O1, where clang-14 runs no loop vectoriser, it is already 2.54× (close to -O2), so lost vectorisation cannot be the main cause. The -O0 drop is the dilution from (a): the -O0 baseline takes 2.392 s against 0.644 s at -O2. The compiler's own report agrees: the hot inner loop is not vectorised in either build at -O2 (D20). D16's explanation is not confirmed; the extra cost has another source.
